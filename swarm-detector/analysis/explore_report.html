4. The important catch: there is no account id technical

The only account-like field is name_orig, a free-text display name. No user id, no username column. 41% of names look like handle DisplayName (e.g. QwertyL1301 ❤❤❤), so the handle is often embedded; others are just first names such as "Сергей" or ".". Different people can share a name, and one person can use several names. Any swarm result built on it is a name-level inference.
  • 15,404 distinct names across 153,225 comments. Median 2 comments per name; 47% of names appear exactly once.
  • Heavy tail: the top name posted 1,548 comments in 7 days; top 10 names produced 7.0% of all comments.
  • Names active in more than one channel: 953 (6.2%). These matter for the "both sides" requirement.

5. Content quality technical

  • Exact duplicate comment texts: 6.9% of comments share their text with another comment. Part is noise: No description (4,437x), pinned channel rules, donation banners, laughing emoji. These must be filtered before using text similarity or you will "find" harmless swarms.
  • Median comment length 43 chars; 75% under 83; max 4,082.
  • Same parent post and same second: 672 (post, second) buckets hold 2+ comments. Max 4 comments in one second on one post: a raw timing signal to test.

6. Where and when technical

Channels are heavily skewed: @RVvoenkor alone has 40% of comments; the top 3 have 80%. Small channels will need per-channel normalisation. Day 13 Oct is a partial day (1,293 rows), so effective window is 14-21 Oct.

7. In plain English simple

What we have: a week of reader comments under posts of 15 Telegram channels about the war, about 153k comments. Each comes with who wrote it (a name only), when (to the second), which channel and which message it answers, plus an English and German translation.

What is great: exact timing, a clear link from each comment to what it replies to, and ready translations. We can ask "who always shows up together, at the same moment, saying the same thing?"

What is tricky:

  • There are no real account ids, only display names. Two "Сергей" might be two people.
  • About 1 in 14 comments is a copy of another, but many are harmless: emoji, channel rules, donation banners. We must clean these out first.
  • Some data is missing: language on about 1 in 6 comments, a few comments without their parent, and no timestamps on pictures and videos.
  • Activity is lopsided: one channel holds 40% of all comments and a handful of names write over a thousand comments each. A lively fan is not a swarm.

What this means for the challenge: we cannot say "this is a bot". We can say "these names keep appearing together, within seconds, with similar words, in several channels", then keep real, angry or very active people out of the list.
